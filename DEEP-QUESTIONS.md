## Working glossary (26 October, following CL639)

- **Tock:** an odd-time trace bit; free in the random-trace model, determined by a particular physical beginning.
- **Seat / depth:** distance from the controlled wall; it is not elapsed time.
- **Referee:** the survival check, pictured as reading successive reconstructed cells; specify the check in use.
- **Twin:** two boundary traces differing in one chosen tock, and their reconstructed backgrounds.
- **Top:** the highest time level where either of two consecutive reconstructed columns still differs.
- **Wake:** background information shaped by earlier choices, which the moving top may encounter again.
- **Fresh:** a leading trace index beyond all previously exposed indices; only fresh odd bits are fair choices in the product model.
- **Prescribed / revisit:** an even leading bit is fixed black; an old leading index is reused. Neither is an independent new coin.
- **Diary:** the ordered trace history; its “free chapter” is the initial free part in the particular reconstruction argument.

**Scope key:** “every trace” marks a deterministic implication under its stated boundary conditions. “Almost surely” means outside a probability-zero set under a named random model. ## How to use this tracker

- Give each question a permanent **DQ number**. Keep the original wording and record later refinements underneath it.
- Explain what an answer would change, what is already known, and the exact point where our understanding stops. Link to evidence instead of repeating an entire technical record.
- When taking up a question, add the reader, date and precise interpretation being examined. On returning it, leave the answer in ordinary words, its mathematical scope, the evidence, and the next unresolved question. Preserve failed attempts.
- Use **Open** for a question awaiting exploration, **Exploring** for a named current investigation, **Parked** for a question awaiting a stated new ingredient, and **Answered in scope** when a specified version has an answer. A refuted proposal can be recorded under an open question; it need not close the larger question.
- There is no question quota, required experiment or obligation to manufacture a breakthrough. Questions may cross problems, but a proposed connection must eventually say what transfers and why.

This tracker is not a launch queue or a proof register. Existing holds and review standards still apply; see [NOW.md](NOW.md). Its creation does not restart workers or schedules. The entries below preserve the conversation and existing evidence; they announce no new theorem or experiment.

## DQ-001 — Changing an exception while preserving its essential behaviour

**Question:** “What can we remove, compress, or replace in a hypothetical Rule 30 exception without destroying the behaviour that makes it an exception?”

**Origin:** proposed in the human–GPT discussion and selected by the owner as a warm starting point, 25 October 2026. **Status: Open.** No investigation is claimed here.

**Why it matters.** If every supposed exception could be made smaller while remaining an exception, and the shrinking could not continue forever, there could be no exception. Alternatively, many different arrangements might be replaceable by a few faithfully representative types.

**Translation.** First name the behaviour being preserved. In our auxiliary test, a finite arrangement beside a permanently white boundary must keep a specified cell black at every second step forever. That is a particular mathematical test, not a synonym for every possible exception to the Rule 30 prize conjecture. A transformation must preserve that test and remain within its allowed arrangements.

**What we know.** Cutting off distant cells preserves only a bounded period of observation unless we prove more. Going backward in time is also not a general shrinking recipe: a row may have no finite predecessor, and the available backward tree does not provide the missing amplification. But preserving the observations we actually constrain can allow changes that preserving every observation would forbid.

**Next useful question.** What information is essential to the observed behaviour, and what merely accompanies it? Name one proposed replacement and the exact observations it preserves before asking whether it makes anything smaller. “Smaller” needs a measure that cannot decrease forever; it need not mean fewer black cells.

**Record:** [amplification review](reports/rstation/GPT-CL637-AMPLIFICATION-REVIEW.md); Cloud's correction and backward-tree bound in CHAT-LEDGER.md, CL638. Related: DQ-004 and DQ-006.

## DQ-003 — The physics beneath the bridge

**Question:** What are we missing underneath the behaviour, as a bridge builder would be missing something essential without understanding gravity, foundations and wind?

**Origin:** the owner's bridge analogy, 25 October 2026. **Status: Open.**

**Translation.** We know Rule 30's local update rule completely. What we lack is a general consequence of it that forces the relevant observation to fail for every finite starting arrangement, however large. Knowing the instruction for each next step is different from understanding what all those steps must eventually do.

**What an answer would change.** A principle about unavoidable influence, incompatible demands, or a quantity that cannot keep changing in the required direction could replace case-by-case inspection. These are possible shapes of explanations, not claims that Rule 30 has an energy or conservation law waiting to be found.

**Next useful question.** What would an eternal survivor have to keep supplying, preventing or coordinating forever? Which of those requirements can we express without assuming the conclusion? Test any proposed principle against known long survivors and certified families, not only typical pictures.

**Record:** [current open gaps](NOW.md); [the research status board](PERIOD-TWO.md), section 6.5.

> **Version of the question examined:** what one-sided feature of the rule could an eternal survivor be fighting against?
>
> **Answer in everyday words (an instinct):** Rule 30 treats its two neighbours unequally. One neighbour enters through "exclusive or":

## DQ-004 — A finite doorway into an infinite problem

**Question:** The eight streets problem reduced infinitely many cases to a few checkable million. Could Rule 30 admit such a reduction?

**Origin:** the owner's comparison, 25 October 2026. **Status: Open.**

**What made eight streets work.** A proved reduction lets a sufficiently large instance surrender its largest burning segment, leaving a smaller instance of the same admissible kind. A solution to the smaller instance extends back to the original. The remaining finite cases were then checked. The reduction, rather than the number of cases checked, is what reaches infinity.

**What must transfer.** We need either a justified reduction from every large bad case to a smaller bad case, or a finite description that retains every feature needed to exclude eternal survival. We do not currently have that for arbitrary Rule 30 tails. Checking more widths does not create it.

**Next useful question.** Could two arrangements be considered equivalent for this question even when their detailed futures differ? If so, prove that the equivalence survives the evolution and preserves the relevant observation. Otherwise a finite model may discard the very influence that matters.

**Record:** [eight-streets reduction](reports/eight-paths-L8.md), section 3; [latest return-map kit review](reports/wolfram-agents/GPT-PORTAL-UPDATE-20261025-EVENING.md). **The scope changes the answer.** In ordinary Rule 30 on the whole line, distinct finite black-cell arrangements against a white background cannot become the same complete row: an outermost difference always forces a difference at the next step. ## DQ-013 — The uneven metronome

**Question:** Could the length of a tick differ from the length of a tock—could our apparently even clock itself be uneven?

**Origin:** the owner's question after GC1889's agreeing-ticks/disagreeing-tocks analysis, 25 October 2026. **Status: the clock distinction is answered; the usefulness of an event-based description is Open.**

**Response.** Rule 30 specifies successive simultaneous updates, not physical seconds. Giving alternating updates different elapsed durations leaves the ordered configurations unchanged; giving different cells different update schedules would instead change the model. The orderly and irregular-looking spatial regions do not by themselves establish different update clocks.

But an update is not necessarily a visible swing. In our description, a tock may leave the watched cell black. Likewise, the third/fifth-cell comparison can remain in agreement at a tock. Counting changes rather than updates therefore gives an uneven event rhythm.

**The exact asymmetry, by hand.** In the third/fifth comparison of GC1889, agreement holds at every even update. Any disagreement is consequently isolated at an odd update: it lasts one update before agreement returns. Between successive disagreements, the agreement lasts an odd positive number of updates—one, three, five, and so on. This lists allowable lengths, not a claim that every length is realized by a finite survivor. The watched cell has the analogous description with black in place of agreement and white in place of disagreement. These are different observables with the same timing constraint; their event sequences must not be identified.

**What compression keeps or loses.** If we tick the clock only when a change happens, agreement/disagreement necessarily alternates. That repeating event word does not imply periodicity in the original update count: it has hidden the waiting times. Retaining the waiting times makes the description informative again. If those waiting times eventually repeated, the original binary sequence would eventually repeat too, by concatenating the repeated finite blocks. Thus, conditional on a finite eternal survivor and GC1889's discrepancy result, the third/fifth disagreement waiting times must also be non-eventually-periodic. There are infinitely many such events under that hypothesis, since a finite number would make the discrepancy eventually constant. No bound on gaps or event density follows.

**Question back.** Are the waiting times constrained by the surrounding cells in a simpler way than the colours themselves? Could a relation between successive waits supply a reduction even though the waits do not repeat? Merely stretching the drawing or discarding the waits cannot do so.

**Record:** [Tick Tock hand note](reports/rule30-tick-tock-hand-analysis.md), with its pending independent algebra read; [G98's established clock-reparametrization distinction](proofs/G98-clocks-lattice-diamonds-and-background-dependent-fronts.md). The run-length argument above is an elementary hand implication, not a measured pattern or a proof of finite survival. GC1890; no computation or clock modification proposed. ## DQ-014 — Can equivalent rules expose a hidden mechanism?

**Question:** The owner encountered the statement that Rule 30 is related by reflection and colour exchange to rules 86, 135 and 149. Can we lean on those related rules to expose something missing in Rule 30?

**Origin:** owner’s question, 26 October 2026. **Status: equivalence answered in scope; usefulness for a simpler description Open.** Recorded at the owner’s request; await Local’s current compute batch before further investigation.

**Answer in everyday words.** These are exact alternative views of the same evolution when the beginning and boundaries are transformed with the rule. Rule 86 reflects left/right; Rule 135 exchanges black/white; Rule 149 does both. Turning a machine around can reveal a connection that was hard to see, even though it remains the same machine. No independent extra evidence or shortcut follows just from having four rule numbers.

**Essential condition.** The single black cell on a white background for Rule 30 corresponds to a single white cell on a black background for Rule 135. The controlled white wall becomes a controlled black wall, and a required black tick becomes a required white tick. A finite black perturbation becomes a finite white perturbation of the transformed background; it must not be confused with finite black support. Reflection reverses the side of the wall and the watched positions. For the symmetric single-cell start on the full line, Rule 86 has exactly Rule 30’s central sequence; with the complemented start, Rule 135 has its colour complement.

**Connection to Tick Tock.** Exchanging both colours preserves equality and inequality of corresponding cells, and preserves the times of colour changes. Reflection transports those statements to the reflected positions. Thus agreement/disagreement and waiting times describe features independent of colour naming across these equivalent systems. This does not prove that those observables evolve autonomously: their next values may still require omitted colours or more distant information. Nor does it identify our auxiliary wall survival condition with the original prize question.

**Question back.** Can we describe what keeps changing using relationships between cells, retaining only a small amount of actual colour information? What information is necessary to predict the next relationship? If the discarded information must be reintroduced at growing cost, the rewrite has not supplied the hoped-for reduction. A useful result would be an exact simpler evolution with the initial/background and boundary conditions retained, together with a consequence for the open survival question.

**Evidence and limits.** The transformation table was checked against the [Wolfram Atlas rule properties](https://atlas.wolfram.com/01/01/30/01_01_1_30.html) and [MathWorld’s Rule 30 entry](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/Rule30.html), 26 October 2026. The preserved-observable statements follow directly by swapping colours or reflecting matching cells. These are elementary consequences of known equivalences, not new theorems or an exhaustive literature review. No dynamics, benchmark, Lean run, public submission or new compute request.

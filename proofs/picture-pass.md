# The picture pass: what would Gareth say?

*Cloud, 2026-10-07, at the owner's request: "please can you go through each of the proofs from where i left off and
give it each everyday picture a challenge pass of 'what would Gareth say'. Although the source pool of my brain
pulls is relatively small, your guessed example already was quite accurate. I will still manual process each one in
my time, but it would be helpful if I continue with the editors rewrite that echos myself first, then i can appraise
if it resonates as a good hit, or whether it's off."*

**How to read it.** The owner's readings ended at C3, so this covers every page from C4 to SP03, 171 pictures. Each
entry gives the picture as it was, Cloud's guess at what Gareth would say (in his voice, but a guess, not his
words), and what the page now says. 86 pictures were rewritten, and the rewrites are already live in
[summaries.md](summaries.md) and on the pages; 85 were guessed "no notes" and are unchanged. The verdict line is
left blank for the owner: hit, off, or his own words. A miss is put back from its "Was" line, or rewritten from the
owner's own reading.

**What the guesses are built from.** The owner's readings of pages 02 to C3. He tests whether the real object
behaves as the picture says: a metronome "is subject to friction and loses energy" (14), a combination lock's dial
"can be turned both ways... What is done can be undone" (20), a fence gap may be "intentional" for a critter (12), a
lamp may be off by a power cut (08). He flags words left undefined: "'the trick' here is poorly defined" (15), "I
don't know what a Sierpinski triangle" (16). He calls out a picture that folds back on itself: "It is an Escher

snippet (02), a synthesiser's attack (11), the car-park timing window (13), notches and ratchets (15), the octave
(09). And he says "no notes" when a picture is concrete: "humans are sensory beings with excellent visual memories -
grounding in real world existential nouns is helpful" (C3).

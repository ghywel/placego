# The fifth frame: a symmetric window for the field

*Design and pre-registration, written 2026-09-04 before any code. The
numbers to beat are stated here so the result cannot be graded after the
fact. Companion to [QUADDIRECTIONAL.md](QUADDIRECTIONAL.md), which this
extends by one slot, and to NFRAME-LIMITS.md section 8, where the case for
the fifth frame was made from the measured flow-error covariance.*

## Why a fifth frame, and why now

The four-frame shader's acceleration is, at uniform spacing, the plain
second difference of the anchor's two adjacent displacements, and its jerk
is the asymmetric stencil (+1, +3, -1) over one composed link. Two things
were measured that a fifth frame fixes (NFRAME-LIMITS.md section 8, "the
fifth frame, re-examined"):

- **Truncation on fast oscillation.** The second difference underreads a
  sinusoid by (2 sin(w/2) / w)^2 - 1: -8.8% at six samples per period,
  2.7 px RMS against O3's 44 px peak. A symmetric quartic over
  displacements at taus -2, -1, +1, +2 corrects it: 3-6x better at eight
  samples per period and fewer. This is the regime the tiny fast-moving
  things a scientist cares about live in.
- **Jerk noise on slow motion.** The symmetric window's jerk stencil has
  coefficients of +/-0.5 on each flow against the asymmetric window's
  (+1, +2, -1), so with the measured correlation (r about 0.35 on textured
  content, 0.9 on the matcher's best) its noise is 0.81 sigma_f against
  2.30 -- 2.8x cleaner at 24 samples per period, 6x at r = 0.9.

The compact four-frame stencil keeps winning jerk below eight samples per
period, where the wider window's own truncation dominates, and it edges the
quartic's acceleration at 24 (0.25 against 0.30 px: the composed flows'
lever arm costs noise when there is no truncation to buy back). So the
fifth frame is chosen per regime, not a replacement -- and the owner's
reason for building it now is that what the wider window teaches may
iterate backwards: a better estimator at N = 5 may say what N = 2 should
have been doing.

## What is built

`quintdirectional-interpolation.glsl`, generated by
`tests/gen_quintdirectional.py` from the same base as the quad (and from any
variant base by the base argument), with five slots 0-4 (`HOOKED`,
`FRAME1`..`FRAME4`; the fourth bind declares `frame_mix_count = 5`, under
libplacebo's ceiling of eight). Everything the quad has, plus:

- luma pyramids and full-res luma for slot 4 (`LUMA_E_*`);
- the cut statistic for slots 3-4;
- the slot 3 <-> 4 flow chains (DE and ED), every level, cached, plus their
  full-res refines -- eight direction chains in all;
- a final pass that, at the exact N:N phase (the output ON slot 2), reads
  four displacements of the anchor: d(-1) and d(+1) directly, d(-2) and
  d(+2) composed from adjacent links exactly as the quad composes its far
  flow (the second link sampled at the first link's landing point), each
  link round-trip checked. The exact quartic through them,
  d(tau) = v tau + a/2 tau^2 + j/6 tau^3 + s/24 tau^4, is solved as a 4x4
  Vandermonde (so variable frame timing is exact); a and j are reported,
  the snap row is solved and ignored (it absorbs the truncation the cubic
  could not). Trust degrades in order: all four links proven -> quartic;
  a far link unproven or severed by a cut -> the quad's cubic on the three
  that remain; an adjacent link unproven -> the tri's quadratic; both ->
  zero, with the same round-trip thresholds and deadbands as the quad.

**The picture does not change.** The 24->60 warp keeps the quad's cubic
placement on the four slots around the output; the fifth frame contributes
to the field, not the picture. That is a regression check, not a
limitation: the interpolation ladder must come out equal to the quad's
(within the ladder's 0.05 dB reproducibility) or the generator has a bug.

## The bind ceiling, and how the fifth slot fits under it

libplacebo binds at most sixteen textures per pass, and the quad's final
pass already binds sixteen (four frames, three cut statistics, three
half-res flows, six full-res flows). A fifth slot adds five. They fit by
packing, in three cheap passes that cost nothing the field can see:

| what | quad | quint, packed | binds saved |
|---|---|---|---|
| frames | 4 | 5 | -1 |
| cut statistics (1x1) | 3 textures | 1 RGBA texture, one gather pass | 3 |
| half-res flows for the straddle read | 3 | 2 RGBA textures, two flows each | 2 |
| full-res flows for the stencil | 6 | 4 RGBA textures, two flows each | 4 |
| **final pass total** | **16** | **12** | |

The packing passes are copies: the half-res ones trivially so, the
full-res ones four passes of two texture reads each. Render time is
measured and stated in the record; the expectation is a few percent over
the quad, of which the extra flow chains are most.

The generators were generalised on 2026-09-03 to carry a base's extra
per-level passes into every pair's chain, so the seeded, propagated and
animation bases produce a quint the same way they produce a quad. The
`shift_pair` machinery gains slot E; `to_fullres` learns to rename
`LUMA_E_H` as it does A-D.

## Pre-registration

Measured at N:N through the same instruments as the quad (`TRI_DIAG`
modes 2, 5 and 7, `accelcheck.py` and its drivers, unchanged), with the
propagated base unless stated:

1. **O3 (the hardest oscillation, six samples per period): the
   acceleration field's RMS error falls from about 2.7 px/interval^2 to
   about 0.5.** The quad's second-difference truncation is the whole of
   that gap; the quartic removes it. Anything above 1.0 means the composed
   +/-2 flows are not trustworthy at that speed and the design's premise
   fails there.
2. **A6 (the jerk null, true jerk identically zero): the jerk field's
   noise floor falls by about 2.8x** (the stencil-coefficient argument at
   r about 0.35); on M1-like content the factor should approach 6.
3. **O1 (24 samples per period): the acceleration RMS does not improve**
   and may worsen by up to 0.05 px (0.25 -> 0.30): the lever-arm cost.
   This is the case that says "choose per regime"; the shader reports both
   estimators in the diagnostic modes so the choice can be measured.
4. **The interpolation ladder equals the quad's on all 32 cases** (within
   0.05 dB).
5. **A7's field at mid speed is not worse than the propagated quad's**
   (26.9% gross velocity, 100% acceleration coverage): the fifth frame adds
   information, it must not add aliases.
6. **Render time:** within 10% of the propagated quad on O5 at 24->60.

What is NOT claimed: any improvement to the picture; any improvement to
jerk below eight samples per period (the compact stencil keeps that);
anything about six frames, which waits on these numbers.

## Costs stated plainly

One more frame of latency at N:N (frame k+2 must exist before the field at
k is reported). Two more flow chains at every level (about +20% of the
quad's pass count). The packing passes. A wider window means a cut inside
it more often: the graded degrade above is the answer, and the cut
statistics are per pair so the degrade is exact.

## Results (2026-09-04, RX 6600, the propagated base unless stated)

**The host first.** The first runs scored 1-2 dB below the quad on the
ladder and produced garbage fields on six of every twenty-four frames. A
probe of the slot times per frame found the cause outside the shader:
ffmpeg's frame queue offers the renderer only the frames within the mixer
kernel's radius (2.0 intervals), which always holds four frames and holds
five only when a boundary frame at exactly two intervals rounds in; when
it rounds out, the hook patch's dispatch gate skips the shader and the
builtin Mitchell mixer draws the frame, silently. The four-frame window was
never affected (probed: stable on every frame). Two host changes followed:
the N:N patch now sizes the queue's lookahead to the declared window for
five frames and more (four keep the radius their published numbers were
measured under), and the hook patch now logs an error on every skipped
window and, with `PL_FRAME_MIX_STRICT` set, paints the frame magenta
instead of blending. With the window stable the quint's 24->60 ladder is
the quad's within 0.06 dB on the five cases checked (item 4).

**The field.** The pre-registered scenes were the wrong instrument at N:N:
a flat white square's field lives on its two edges, and they disagree
(the leading edge measures the object, the trailing edge the background it
uncovers), which the human-reading view showed as a square painted half
red, half cyan; and O3's 12 px at six samples per period puts the +/-2
displacements beyond the search reach. Two textured scenes were added for
the regime the fifth frame is for -- `O9_osc_tex_fast` (6 px, eight
samples per period) and `O10_osc_tex_tiny` (4 px, six) -- and checked as
ground truth. Scored with `accelcheck.py` at N:N over frames 4-20, RMS of
the box mean against both the discrete second difference (what a
four-frame stencil measures by construction) and the continuous
derivative (what the quartic estimates), in px per interval squared:

| scene | samples/period | quad vs discrete | quad vs continuous | quartic vs continuous | gain |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| O6 textured gentle | 24 | 0.029 | 0.034 | 0.037 | none, as predicted |
| O9 textured fast | 8 | 0.035 | 0.137 | 0.050 | 2.7x |
| O10 textured tiny | 6 | 0.022 | 0.276 | 0.041 | 6.7x |

The quad reads the discrete difference exactly and the discrete
difference is the wrong number: on O10 its 0.276 is the -8.8% truncation
of section 8's formula on a 4.4 px/interval^2 peak, to the second decimal.
The quartic removes it. Item 1 (3-6x at eight samples per period and
fewer) is met on the scene it was about.

**The jerk floor.** A6, true jerk zero: per-texel std of the jerk field
over the interior, median over frames 4-20 -- quad 0.592, quint quartic
0.202, the quint's own cubic mode 0.595. A factor 2.93 (item 2: 2.8).

**A7.** Velocity field 26.9% gross on the mid-speed frames, the same as
the propagated quad (item 5 met for velocity); the acceleration field 40%
gross against the quad's 34%, with 100% coverage in both -- the quartic's
composed +/-2 links carry the lattice residue into the acceleration on
that texture. Not met for acceleration; recorded.

**Time.** O5 at 24->60, 60 frames, median of three: quad 4.75 s, quint
5.60 s, +18% (item 6 asked for 10%). The two extra flow chains are the
cost and the packing passes are a small part of it. The shader is a field
instrument; for the picture the quad is the right file.

**Modes 8 and 9** report the four-frame cubic from the same anchor, so a
reading can choose per regime as section 8 said it should: the compact
stencil wins jerk below eight samples per period and edges acceleration at
24; the quartic wins acceleration at eight and below and jerk on slow
motion.

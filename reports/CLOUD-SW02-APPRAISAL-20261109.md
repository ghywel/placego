## 4. What our work since has changed about the swarm's items

- **Door 1, the trichotomy (GPT correction, GC2792).** The retained X/Y graphs and tested periodic words are finite results. Completed tested periodic E-words die at finite widths; the4,16 and4,20 cases remained unresolved cap stops in CL755. This does not show that every periodic E-word dies or that lineage death is only a compute wall. In CL758's20,000-row cohort,98.8% of complete E blocks contain trap entry; that is not98.8% of all trap entries. The other3.5% of rows have long finite period2/7 suffixes, without certificates of permanent locks. Retrospective cohort rates do not establish flat hazards or memorylessness. Thus neither universal capture nor the claimed permanent three-way classification follows from this sample. These are the scoped corrections already carried in GC2783–GC2789, which this appraisal must retain. TrapOrPeriodic remains open.

## 7. Verdict

The swarm was not wasted. Its priority list was executed to the end and produced seven posted or ready modules and the whole bridge thread, and its record repair paid off twice inside a week. What is being wasted is the long tail: ninety-five AJAR doors with named tests never run, ninety-four doors and thirty barrier statements nobody has read, seven submissions already clear or already carried and not started, fifty-seven findings and fifty-six open questions that no page points at, a backlog whose 327 open boxes have no owner, and compiled files with 57, 48 and 276 truncated fields. None of that needs compute. It needs two or three desk sessions and a harvest ledger, then the cheap submissions, then the carries. On the general case the swarm's own forecast stands: nothing prize-level, and "the count column is the whole wall"; the one reduction worth a block is P2-2.1's energy hypothesis, which the sheet names and nobody has picked up.

### 45: what cheap dyadic lags cannot cover

Read original P2-2 section7(b), SKM-P2-2-3(b) and SKR-P2-2-3(b).
The shortest4-point obstruction is residues modulo3: a power of2 is never0
modulo3, so a dyadic-difference clique has at most3 vertices. This only rules out
shift sets whose *every* difference is cheap.

The weighted extension is carried as follows. Let A be adjacency on Z for
differences plus/minus powers of2, nu a probability distribution of finite
support, M=max nu, and w=sum_x nu(x)(A nu)(x). Distinct vertices have at most4
common neighbors. Indeed a common neighbor gives an ordered representation
of their positive separation as a sum of two signed powers: at most2 same-sign
positive representations and at most2 opposite-sign representations. Then

`w^2 <= sum_x nu(x)(A nu)(x)^2 <= M + 4M = 5M`.

The diagonal contribution uses sum nu(v)^2(A nu)(v)<=M; the off-diagonal uses
the common-neighbor bound. Hence dyadic pairs have vanishing weight when
M tends to0. Without this diffuseness guard the statement is false (two atoms
at separation1 retain positive weight). This does not rule out a vdC argument
that also controls non-dyadic lags. The sharper extremal/weighted claims in the
record skeptic are not needed or promoted. The modulo3 observation and generic
vdC/Cauchy-Schwarz method are prior art; this read asserts no novelty priority.

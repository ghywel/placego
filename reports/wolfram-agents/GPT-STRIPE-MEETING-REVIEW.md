## Relation and coordinate convention

Write `A_t=phiRows a t`, where

    Phi(a) = a XOR ((a << 1) OR (a << 2)).

The observed bit is `A_t[c+t]`. All rows and centre indices are natural numbers.
`StripeWord` is the LOW-bit-first language `0*(1|11)(01)*`, not its reversal.
`StripeRelated(a,b,c)` says either a=b, or there is ONE common cut j<=c with:

- both prefixes on bits0..j in that language and both bitj black;
- both bit(j+1) white;
- equality at every bit i>j+1.

The white separator and common cut cannot be omitted. TailInvariant and the
five-state parser establish both directions of the grammar interpretation;
pending/valid/identity flags are then proved equivalent to these cut conditions.
`relationRun_full_iff` additionally requires zero tails above n and c+1<n.
It is not an equivalence relation generated by arbitrary independent cuts,
and the source does not identify it with a numerical normalizer.

**Hand coordinate check, not a formal mapping lemma from this module:** for
physical rule `left XOR (centre OR right)`, bit i at time t represents position
t-i, with initial bit i at position -i. At position t+1-i the three preceding
indices are i, i-1, i-2, giving exactly Phi (negative indices are white).
Thus bit c+t observes the fixed physical position -c. Treating initial bit i
as position +i would reflect the rule incorrectly.

Unexpected adversarial check: a=5,b=6,c=2 satisfy the relation at j=2,
with low prefixes 101 and 011, common white bit3 and zero higher tails.
By hand Phi(5)=27 and Phi(6)=26, so both observed bit3 are black. A naive
positive-position encoding would instead give different next bits at +2
(0 XOR 1 versus 1 XOR 1). Under the correct reflection both configurations
have left edge -2; there is no conflict with the edge-separation results.
In general a nontrivial related pair has the same highest set-bit index:
both have black bitj, white bitj+1 and identical higher bits. This is a hand
scope guard, not a classification of arbitrary equal-trace pairs.

## Why the finite certificate covers unbounded sizes

The machine reads all eight possible letters (row-a bit, row-b bit, centre

bits of each original row, and marker progress. `first_exact`/`second_exact`
identify the computed bits with Phi/Phi², including the guarded lower boundary.
View.Valid and decode/encode proofs prevent packing fields from overlapping.

The certificate checks initial membership, closure under EVERY accepted
letter, and an end predicate at every pool node. `all_of_member` transports
the node predicate along the successful search path; its soundness needs no
unmentioned sortedness premise. `run_member` inducts over an arbitrary finite
word. The number2321 is a state-pool size, not a row-size or time cutoff.

Rejected inputs must still be accounted for. A second centre marker is
rejected; so are unequal first or second centre observations. For actual rows
with those two observations equal, `actualView_advance` and `actualInput_run`
prove a successful run at every length. Hence rejection does not silently
remove a valid pair to which the theorem is applied.

For arbitrary a,b,c, the proof chooses the loose support bound n=a+b+c+1.
Initial rows are zero at indices>=n; Phi grows this bound by at most2, and
Phi² by at most4. `flush_actual` identifies five appended zero letters with
the actual zero tails; n+5 also lies past every relevant cut and separator.
All three final accepts therefore mean the complete row relation, rather
than mere finite-prefix compatibility. Marker progress is nonzero because
c<n, so the end predicate's unmarked-input escape branch is inapplicable.

`endGood` excludes accepting ONLY the time2 relation while rejecting time0
and time1. It yields `two_step_common_cut`, then `two_step_stripe_related`:

    equal observations at times0,1 and relation at time2

## Final statements and dependency audit

`stripe_meeting_is_early` uses strong induction on t: pull a relation at t>=2
back to t-2 or t-1 using the two-step result and the corresponding two equal
observations. With equality at EVERY earlier s<t, any finite meeting implies
a meeting at time0 or1. It assumes neither one-step invariance nor eventual
entry into the relation. `no_delayed_stripe_meeting` is its contrapositive.

The second checked predicate, `preservesTwo_pool`, gives an independent
direction: relation at time0 implies relation at time2. `center_pair` gives
the first two equal observations. Together, two-step preservation and strong
induction give `stripe_related_same_trace`, equality at EVERY observed time.
These dependencies do not use trace completeness or early meeting to prove
preservation; no carrying circularity found.

Hand negative control, also identified in the source: rows2 and3 at c=1 are
related at cut1. Phi(2)=14 and Phi(3)=13 are not related at c=2: possible cut2
fails because bit3 is black, cut1 fails a black-bit condition, and cut0 fails
the other black-bit condition. Therefore one-step preservation really is false;
replacing two steps by one would invalidate the argument.

Finally, `eventually_stripe_iff_early` assumes ENTIRE trace equality and proves

    (exists a finite meeting) iff (relation at time0 OR time1).

**It does not prove the left-hand existence statement.** The reverse implication
merely chooses t=0 or1. Equal traces without any such meeting are not excluded.
Thus no complete trace-kernel classification, EdgeObservable, or prize result

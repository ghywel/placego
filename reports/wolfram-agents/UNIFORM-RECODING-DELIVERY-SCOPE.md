## Exact older claims to distinguish

For C>=0, a finite word satisfies the repeat inequality if every repetition with positive lag q,
indices a<=s<=b, and b+q within the supplied horizon obeys

    b <= 2a+q+C.

The old G135 claim says that EVERY standard half-open irrational mechanical prefix through N=251(C+4)
violates this inequality, uniformly over slope and phase. Its proof does not assume bounded partial quotients:
the finite comparison deadlines themselves bound the usable continued-fraction scales. G133's golden-angle
constant84 and G134's bounded-type constant8(A+1)^4+3 are narrower quantitative variants.

G136 extends the finite-prefix statement to rational/endpoint slopes by finite-prefix perturbation, and
then to a binary recoding of width w+1 with the EXPLICIT horizon

    H(C,w)=251(C+4)+250w.

No injectivity of the recoding is required. The width guard is essential: increasingly wide recodings can
fit arbitrary finite target prefixes. The old proof and L089 note explicitly retain this counter-control.
Orbit-related arc endpoints can be represented by such recodings with width controlled by the span of
their orbit exponents, not merely the number of endpoints. Arbitrary unrelated endpoints are not included.

Under a genuine Rule30 clock from time0 and initial left-zero radius L>=1, physical restart at even time2a
retains the clock phase and gives left-zero radius at most L+2a. Thus matching pieces obey
b-a<H(L+2a,w). For reset times t_j and uniformly bounded width w, the old bound is

    t_(j+1) <= 503t_j+251L+1004+250w.

Functions, phases and slopes may change between pieces. Against a fixed recoded base, disagreement times
instead have the first bound k_0<=251(L+4)+250w and

    k_(j+1) <= 503k_j+251L+1507+250w.

No positive disagreement density, entropy, realizability, arbitrary-width horizon or prize claim follows.
The restart/finite comparison/coding-endpoint steps must survive exact formalization; the recorded hand
second reads are not a present current-toolchain certificate of these formulas.

## The question

Problem 1 of the Rule 30 Prize asks whether the center column of rule 30 is ever eventually periodic. Its first case is period 2, the clock `0101…`. Suppose some configuration keeps the clock on its center column for ever. Rule 30 is `left XOR (center OR right)`: the cell left of the clock is determined by the clock and the cell right of it, so the whole configuration is determined by its right half, and the right half is driven by the clock at its boundary. How much freedom does the column beside the clock, column `1`, keep? Our submission R30-20261023-4767e0 answered with a certified number at window width `12`: read at the even times (the *visible bits*, the ones that feed back into column `-1`), column `1` carries about `0.258` bits of information per bit. This essay takes the next rung, width `14`: at most `300 · 1.184655442^n` words of length `n`, an asymptotic rate of `log₂ 1.184655442 ≈ 0.24447` bits per bit with a finite correction `log₂ 300 / n`. The point of the essay is as much the method as the number: the width-12 proof rebuilt the automaton inside one kernel computation, and that does not scale; here the states are explicit data and the kernel checks them one theorem at a time.

## The window automaton

Take the `M` cells right of the center as a window. Under the clock, one time step maps the window row `s` to `nxt[s, tv, u]`: the clock bit `tv` enters from the left and the unknown cell beyond the window, `u`, from the right. Two steps, with the clock white then black, give `step2[s, u, v]`, with the same exterior cell at two successive times as the free inputs `u, v`. The set of window rows consistent with the visible bits read so far is a state; reading the next visible bit `b` keeps the rows with cell `1` equal to `b` and takes all their images under both inputs at both steps. This subset construction is deterministic in `b`, so the number of visible words of length `n` is at most the number of paths of length `n` from the root, and that grows like the spectral radius of the graph. At width `6` the automaton has `31` states, `18` dead edges and growth rate `1.3580`; at width `8`, `56` states and `1.2801`:

```wl
nxt[m_][s_, tv_, u_] := BitAnd[BitXor[BitOr[BitShiftLeft[s, 1], tv], BitOr[s, BitShiftRight[s, 1], BitShiftLeft[u, m - 1]]], 2^m - 1];
automaton[m_] := Module[{nx = nxt[m], root = Range[0, 2^m - 1], states, index, succ, q = 1, cur, l1, l2},
   states = {root}; index = <|root -> 1|>; succ = {};
   While[q <= Length[states], cur = states[[q]];
    AppendTo[succ, Table[
      l1 = Union @@ Table[If[BitAnd[s, 1] == b, {nx[s, 0, 0], nx[s, 0, 1]}, {}], {s, cur}];
      l2 = Union @@ Table[{nx[t, 1, 0], nx[t, 1, 1]}, {t, l1}];
      If[l2 === {}, 0, If[KeyExistsQ[index, l2], index[l2], AppendTo[states, l2]; index[l2] = Length[states]]], {b, 0, 1}]];
    q++];
   {states, succ}];
growth[succ_, its_] := Module[{n = Length[succ], v, w, lam}, v = ConstantArray[1., n];
   Do[w = ConstantArray[0., n]; Do[If[succ[[i, b]] > 0, w[[succ[[i, b]]]] += v[[i]]], {i, n}, {b, 2}]; lam = Total[w]/Total[v]; v = w/Total[w], {its}]; lam];
Table[With[{a = automaton[m]}, {m, Length[a[[1]]], Count[Flatten[a[[2]]], 0], growth[a[[2]], 300]}], {m, {6, 8}}]
```

## The certificate at width 14

## The idea of the proof

Three things make the certificate a theorem.

1. **The window follows the kit's rule.** `win x t` packs the cells `1 … M` of `evol x t` into a number, cell `p + 1` as bit `p`. One step of rule 30 on the configuration is the bit formula `nxt` on the window (`win_succ`), with the clock bit on the left and the cell beyond the window on the right; two steps under the clock are `step2` (`win_step2`).
2. **The kernel checks each state.** The module carries the bitset of every state, the graph and the weights. For state `i`, `stateCheck` sweeps the state's bitset in `64`-bit words, finds each member by its top bit, computes the member's four two-step images and tests each against the bitset of the successor of the member's label (`imgs4In`, a shift and a mask). A dead edge is the empty bitset, so a member on that label fails the test. `stateCheck_spec` says what a passing check means for every member below `2^M`; `explicit_sound` assembles the `919` state theorems into the closure of every edge, with the successor live whenever a member needs it. The root is checked to be the full bitset.
3. **The count.** Along the visible word the actual window row stays in the current state's bitset (`st_inv`), so the visible word is accepted (`visFrom_mem`); the accepted words of length `n` from state `i` number `cntA`, and `D^n · wmin · cntA ≤ R^n · w_i` by induction on `n` from the weight inequality (`weight_bound`). With `w_0 ≤ 300 · wmin` this is the headline.

Why one theorem per state. The width-12 submission rebuilt every state inside a single kernel computation; run at width `14` that computation had not finished after `53` minutes and held `10` GB of memory. The Lean kernel keeps its caches of reduced terms for the lifetime of one declaration and discards them with it, and the prize verifier's replay sends each theorem to the kernel separately; so the `919` theorems `s14_0 … s14_918`, each a `decide +kernel` on its own state, keep the memory bounded, and the whole module checks in about four minutes on Cloud's machine. The price is the explicit data: about `4.5` MB of decimal literals. The design is GPT's (GPT-CL601-WIDTHS.md); the per-row cost is the same as before, about `0.8` ms, with `193,314` row occurrences at width `14`.

## What is not proved

- Nothing decides `Q01` or Problem 1. The theorem bounds how many visible words a clocked configuration can show; a bound below `1` bit per bit does not say the number of configurations is zero.
- The bound is for the clock from time `0`, for every configuration `ℤ → Bool`, finite or not. It is a statement about the hypothetical clocked configurations, not about the single seed, whose center column is not the clock. The eventual form (the clock from an even time on) is proved in the width-12 module and not repeated here.
- Width `14` is what the module proves. Width `16` (about `0.2131` bits, GPT's certificate) would need about `45` MB of state literals in this form and is not attempted.
- The state bitsets are data, checked, not derived: any family of sets with the root full and every edge closed gives an upper bound, and the kernel never relies on the sets being the ones the search produced.
- The weights are a certificate, not the spectral radius: the ratio `1.184655442` is slightly above the growth rate `1.1844186`, by design.

## Provenance

The channel bound is the Wolfram · Collatz · Mahler record's candidate 2 for the prize, proved at width 12 in submission R30-20261023-4767e0 by a checker that rebuilt the automaton inside one kernel computation. Run at width 14 that computation did not finish; GPT read the Lean 4.35.0 kernel's source and the verifier's replay and specified the present design, explicit state bitsets with one closure theorem per declaration (GPT-CL601-WIDTHS.md, 2026-10-23); Cloud (Claude) implemented it the same day, with a pilot at width 6 whose negative controls (a false dead edge, swapped labels, a dropped image) are rejected as they must be. The width-14 certificate was computed by the same Collatz-Wielandt routine as before; this essay's Wolfram Language cells regenerate the automaton at widths 6 and 8 and compute the width-14 diagnostics (the weight inequality, the growth rate, the accepted-word counts) from the supplied graph, independently of the kernel's run; they do not regenerate the width-14 graph.

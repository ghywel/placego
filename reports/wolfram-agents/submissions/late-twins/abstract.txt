In the zero-boundary left half of rule 30, read as natural numbers with one tick F and two ticks G = F∘F, Q01 says that every natural reaches an even value under G. We prove in core Lean that for every nonzero natural y and every t ≥ 46, the row x = F^(t-2)(y) has a twin: a different natural x′ of the same width, with the same cell beside the wall, and with F²(x′) = F²(x). If y never reaches an even value and t is even, the twin never does either and is not on the orbit of y. The top of a row evolves on its own, by an explicit map R, so any twenty-cell band with its top cell black settles on one four-cycle within 26 ticks; the kernel checks this in two stages of 4,096 and 512 cases. Every phase of that cycle carries reset words 0 1 0 or 0 1111 0 of the backward transducer, so inverting twice with one cell flipped returns a finite row. Twins share their future after two ticks. Nothing here decides Q01.

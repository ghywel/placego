Rule 30's right edge, read in the frame that moves with it, is the map x_j -> x_j XOR (x_(j-1) OR x_(j-2)) on binary sequences. Its first n bits repeat with period 2^E(n), and the kit proves E(n) <= ceil(n/2) + Q_0(floor(n/2)), where Q_0 grows like an iterated logarithm (`Rule30RightPeriods.per_upper_half`). We prove that this upper bound does not depend on OR. Take any family of two-input gates g_j, one per depth, and any seed, finite or infinite, and let bit j evolve by x_j(t+1) = x_j(t) XOR g_j(x_(j-1)(t), x_(j-2)(t)), missing bits white. The exponent obtained by lifting one bit at a time is exactly that of the least period (`Rule30Submission.prefix_period_iff`), and every window of bits obeys the kit's record-charge bound E(a+2m) - E(a) <= m + Q_(E(a))(m) <= m + Q_0(m) (`Rule30Submission.prefix_window_bound`, `Rule30Submission.prefix_window_le_B`, `Rule30Submission.prefixExp_le_B`). If every gate is affine, the first n bits have period dividing 2^r whenever n + 1 <= 2^r (`Rule30Submission.affine_prefix_bound`). The reason is that a gate's derivative in its newer input is constant, s or NOT s, so one more finite difference removes it, and the kit's reset and countdown laws need nothing else. No lower bound, doubling density or statement about the centre column is claimed.

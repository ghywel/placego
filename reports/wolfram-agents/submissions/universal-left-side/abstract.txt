Rowland (2006, section 5) conjectured that rule 30 has only one left side: from any row with a leftmost black cell, the eventual period of each left diagonal is independent of the row. He located the first possible failure at his column 53,209, where an eventually white diagonal has an even-weight left neighbour. We prove the conjecture through that column's predecessor, in the kit's reset framework, with one common time shift. A strip is admissible through J when its base words are black, each word obeys the reset recurrence of the two below, and at every wheel the nearer word has blacks arbitrarily late, or some period L of every lower word is an antiperiod of the child; Rowland's odd-parity condition gives the antiperiod. Theorem (universal_of_strip; Lean, standard axioms): for every array obeying the left-diagonal recurrence with a black edge, hence for every configuration with a leftmost black cell (one_left_side), and every admissible strip, one time shift aligns every wheel through J with the strip from some time on. The induction is Rowland's: a black of the nearer word forces the child; otherwise both children integrate the farther word, and their constant difference, if one, is the antiperiod's shift, which the lower strip does not see. Corollaries: any two such configurations share one left side up to a shift; the seed's diagonals are the strip's, so the seed's strip is certified without evolving it. The seed's strip is admissible through 53,207, with white words at wheels 2, 7, 28, 399; wheel 53,207 has even weight, the first fork. Nothing concerns the center column.

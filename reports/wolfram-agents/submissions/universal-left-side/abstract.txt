Rowland (2006, section 5) conjectured that rule 30 has only one left side: from any row with a leftmost black cell, the eventual period of each left diagonal is independent of the row. He located the first possible failure at his column 53,209, where an eventually white diagonal has an even-weight left neighbour. We prove the conjecture below that column,, with one common time shift. A strip is admissible below a cut J when its base words are black, each word obeys the reset recurrence of the two below, and at every wheel the nearer parent has blacks arbitrarily late, or is white with the farther parent of odd parity over a common period of the lower strip. Theorem (Lean, standard axioms): for every admissible strip and every configuration with a leftmost black cell, one time shift aligns every wheel below J with the strip. The induction is Rowland's: a black of the nearer parent forces the child; a white parent makes the child an integrator whose two continuations differ by a shift the lower strip does not see. Corollaries: any two such configurations share one left side below J up to a shift; the seed's diagonals are the strip's, so the seed's strip is certified without evolving it. The strip generated from 1, 1 by the recurrence alone is admissible below 53,208: white words at wheels 2, 7, 28, 399 with odd farther weights; wheel 53,207 has weight 6, the first fork. Nothing concerns the center column.

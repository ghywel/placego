Rowland (2006, section 5) conjectured that rule 30 has only one left side: from any row with a leftmost black cell, the eventual period of each left diagonal is independent of the row. He located the first possible failure at his column 53,209, an eventually white diagonal with an even-weight left neighbour, leaving open whether a second continuation occurs. We settle both sides. Theorem universal_left_side (Lean, standard axioms): for every configuration with a leftmost black cell, one time shift aligns every left diagonal through wheel 53,207 with the seed's strip from some time on; any two such configurations share one left side up to a shift, and the seed's strip is certified without evolving it. Theorem first_fork: two finite rows, the seed's row at tick 72,000 on 53,209 bits and the same row with bit 53,208 flipped, agree on every wheel through 53,207 and are complements at wheel 53,208, at every tick, and no shift identifies them. Universality stops exactly where Rowland said, and his second continuation occurs. The generic step is his induction in the kit's reset framework (universal_of_strip): a black of the nearer word forces the child; otherwise both children integrate the farther word, and a constant difference of one is the antiperiod's shift, invisible to the lower strip. The data are sixteen rows: the strip's 16-tick return, the white wheels 2, 7, 28, 399 with their period and antiperiod checks, and the even weight 6 at wheel 53,206, all kernel-checked.

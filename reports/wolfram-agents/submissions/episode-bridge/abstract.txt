Read a Rule 30 row from its rightmost black cell, as the kit's Rule30FrozenRuler does: an edge code, updated by the edge map. A phase is a family of depth columns that obeys the edge map and repeats in time with period p. An episode at depths k, k + 1 is a run of ticks on which those two depths of a black-edge code follow the phase. We prove in core Lean, on the kit's objects, that every depth has a finite longest episode if and only if no eternal point of any period follows the phase at every depth from some depth on. This bridge was proposed in our record by Claude (Anthropic), working as the record's Cloud worker; here it is formal, with two sharpenings. First, a finite criterion: an episode of lcm(2^B(k+2), p) ticks lasts for ever, where 2^B(k+2) is the kit's uniform period bound for the first k + 2 bits (Rule30GateClocks), so the longest episode at depth k is finite exactly when no episode reaches that horizon, and is then below it. Second, an eventual tail is an eternal one: a pair that follows the phase from some time on gives a periodic black-edge point (a pasted code) that follows it at every time. The proof needs no pigeonhole; the uniform prefix period replaces it. As controls, for the kit's eight-tick ether both sides of the bridge are the kit's own theorems (episode_bound_all, no_eventual_ether_pair), and the rain code's orbit gives a phase whose episodes are unbounded. The kit's checker passes with standard axioms only. Nothing here decides finiteness for any new phase, and nothing concerns the centre column or the Prize problems.

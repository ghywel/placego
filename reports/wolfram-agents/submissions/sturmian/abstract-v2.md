For every directive sequence $d$ with $d_n \ge 1$, the characteristic Sturmian word $c_d$ of slope $[0; d_0 + 1, d_1, d_2, \ldots]$ is not the sequence of visible bits of any configuration with a leftmost black cell whose center column reads the clock $0101\ldots$ from time $0$: if $x_0(-L)$ is black, $x_0$ is white left of $-L$, $\mathrm{evol}\,x_0\,t\,0 = [t \text{ odd}]$ for all $t$ and $\mathrm{evol}\,x_0\,(2i)\,1 = c_d(i)$ for all $i$, then false (`Rule30Submission.characteristic_excluded`). The same holds for the complement of $c_d$, for every block code $i \mapsto f(c_d(i), \ldots, c_d(i + r - 1))$ of $c_d$ (`characteristic_block_excluded`), and for the Fibonacci word (`fibonacci_excluded`).

The catalog reduces period 2 to `Rule30ZeroBoundary.Q01` and has no result about which right neighbours a clocked center column admits. Our submission R30-20261022-91b0b0 proved Corollary F: the visible bits of such a configuration have no near-squares at unbounded scales. This essay supplies a family of words that do have them.

The standard words $s_0 = 1$, $s_1 = 0$, $s_{n+2} = s_{n+1}^{d_n} s_n$ are prefixes of $c_d$. Two list inductions give the classical near-commutation, $s_{n+1} s_n$ and $s_n s_{n+1}$ agree except in their last two letters (`std_near_comm`), so $c_d$ repeats its prefix of length $|s_{n+1}|$ with slack $2$ at every $n$ (`cw_near_square`), the near-squares Corollary F forbids.

Nothing is claimed for Sturmian words that are not characteristic, nor for any other right neighbour; the submission R30-20261023-234c71 treats every slope and intercept.

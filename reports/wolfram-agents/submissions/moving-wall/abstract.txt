Insert one black cell, with white to its right, into a spatially periodic Rule 30 background and follow the leftmost cell where the evolution differs from the background. On the kit's classic ether this front stands still. We prove in core Lean that on two other ring backgrounds it moves at an exact nonzero speed: on 100100000 (temporal period 57) exactly 5/19 cell per tick, and on 1010000 (temporal period 63, alignment (0, 4)) exactly 2/9. For every t >= 300 there is an anchor A with the background intact at and left of A and a difference within the next 24 (respectively 16) cells, and -14 <= 19 (A + 72) + 5 (t - 300) <= 22, respectively -20 <= 9 (A + 55) + 2 (t - 300) <= 4. The proof is a closed set of window states with a moving anchor (498 and 74 states) whose closure under both values of the cell entering from the right is checked by the kernel on bit masks, together with an exact integer potential on its edges, a soundness theorem for one tick on every Rule 30 evolution of the whole line, and a bit-mask simulation that puts the inserted edge into the closure at t = 300. Since the closure accepts every input from the right, the speed belongs to the front and the background, not to the damage behind it, and the same laws hold for every right tail beyond x = 252, respectively 261. On 1010000 the speed attains the universal healing bound. The kit's checker passes with standard axioms only. Nothing here concerns the centre column of the single-cell pattern or the Prize problems themselves.

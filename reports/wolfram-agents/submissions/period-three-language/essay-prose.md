# The Column-One Language Beside a Period-Three Center Weakens the Left Statements That Exclude Period Three

Gareth Herbert; Claude (Anthropic); GPT (OpenAI). 8 November 2026.

## 1. The statement

If the center column of rule 30 is eventually periodic with least period `3`, then from some time `T` on it repeats `001` or `011` (`Rule30EtherThreeLeft.normalize_period_three`). The kit reduces each case to an open statement about the left half alone.

- **`C3s001`** (`Rule30EtherThreeSkeleton`): no finitely supported zero-boundary left configuration keeps column `-1` equal to `(0, *, 1)` at the phases `0, 1, 2` for all large times.
- **`C3w w011`** (`Rule30EtherThreeLeft`): no finitely supported left configuration, run with column `0` replaced by the effective boundary `010`, keeps column `-1` equal to `(*, 0, 1)` for all large times.

The starred phase is free. Along the true history, column `-1` there is the complement of column `1` (`col_m1_001_skeleton`, `col_m1_011`), and column `1` belongs to the right half. The left statements ignore this: they must exclude every free word.

This module shows that the right half constrains the free word, and it weakens the left statements accordingly. All results concern the seed configuration, as the kit's reductions do.

- `col1_011`: if the center is `011`-periodic from `T`, then from some time on column `1`, read at phase `0` of consecutive periods, never spells `00`, `011110`, `111010`, `0101010`, `0101110`, `01011010`, `11101110` or `111011010`.
- `col1_001`: if the center is `001`-periodic from `T`, then from some time on column `1`, read at phase `1`, never spells `010`, `101`, `11111`, `000000`, `100111`, `111001`, `00000111`, `01110001`, `10011001` or `11100000`. So its runs of black have length `2` to `4`, and its runs of white length `2` to `5`.
- `free_bits_011`, `free_bits_001`: column `-1` never spells the complements, for example `11` and `100001` beside `011`.
- **`C3r011`**, the restriction of `C3w w011` to left configurations whose free bits avoid those complements, still excludes the tail `011^∞` (`not_eventually_011_of_C3r`). Likewise **`C3g001`**, the restriction of `C3s001`, excludes `001^∞` (`not_eventually_001_of_C3g`).
- `no_eventual_period_three_of_C3g`: together, `C3g001` and `C3r011` exclude an eventually `3`-periodic center.
- `C3r011_of_C3w`, `C3g001_of_C3s`: the kit's statements imply the new ones. `C3r011` and `C3g001` are open.

## 2. The strip graph and the bad class

Fix the center word `w` and a width `b`. The strip is the list of columns `1..b` of the right half. Its successor is determined by the center cell and by the cell of column `b + 1`, which the strip graph leaves free. A vertex is a phase and a strip, with one edge for each value of the free cell. From time `T` on, the true right half is a walk in this finite graph, so it eventually stays in one strongly connected component.

The kit's bad-class certificates (`Rule30PeriodFourSkeleton.Cert`) rank the vertices so that ranks never rise along an edge. In every rank class except one, column `1` at the white phases is a function of a counter that advances at those phases. If the walk ended in such a class, column `1` would be eventually periodic at the white times, which masked Jen forbids. So the walk ends in the exempt class, the bad class (`Cert.sound_bad`).

## 3. Reading paths at period three

`Rule30PeriodTwoLanguage` reads the paths inside the bad class at period `2`. This module does the same at period `3`. `occurs3 C bad R pat` is a subset simulation over bitmasks of strips. It starts from the strips of phase `R` in the bad class whose column `1` reads the first letter. Each further letter takes three ticks, over all eight values of the free cells, keeping only the strips that stay in the bad class at every tick and read that letter. `no_pattern3` is its soundness: if `occurs3 C bad R pat = false` and the strip stays in the bad class from `T'` on, then column `1` at the times `t, t + 3, ...` (`t ≥ T'` of phase `R`) does not spell `pat`.

## 4. Two certificates

The certificate for `011` has strip width `7` and `384` vertices. Its bad class is the one cyclic component of `136` vertices whose column `1` is not periodic. The certificate for `001` has width `10` and `3072` vertices, with a bad class of `553`. Both certificates were generated by computer, and the kernel checks them with `Cert.checkb` and `Cert.checkGb`. One `decide` per word list checks that no listed word occurs. Further `decide` checks show that the control words `11` and `0110111` (beside `011`) and `11`, `0011` and `110011` (beside `001`) do occur. So the bad classes carry real paths, and the exclusions are not vacuous.

Wider strips forbid more words. The word `1001` beside `001` first becomes forbidden at width `13`, and the language keeps shrinking slowly to width `22` (computation, not in the Lean). The widths here are the smallest at which the stated run bounds hold.

## 5. The left statements

The reduction runs as the kit's reductions of `C3w` and `C3s001` do. Take the seed's own row at time `T` as the left configuration; it is finitely supported. Through the effective boundary (`Rule30DrivenLeft.cell_eq_L_effective`), its column `-1` is the true column `-1`. That column fits the skeleton, and by `free_bits_011` or `free_bits_001` its free bits avoid the complements. So a tail would contradict `C3r011` or `C3g001`.

## 7. Prior art and scope

The method is the kit's.
- Bad-class strip certificates with masked Jen (`Rule30PeriodFourSkeleton`), used at period `3` for the skeletons of `Rule30EtherThreeSkeleton`.
- The subset simulation of `Rule30PeriodTwoLanguage` at period `2`, with additions by G.P.J.W. Quint and Gemini.
- The reductions follow `not_eventually_011_of_C3w` and `not_eventually_001_of_C3s`.

The external Lean development [`lhallee/rule30-open`](https://github.com/lhallee/rule30-open) treats period `3` as well.
- Its `ThreeRuns` proves that beside `001` column `1` at the second white step avoids `010`, `101` and `1001`. That holds for every right half, finite or not, so it covers a larger population than ours and one more word.
- Its `ConstrainedLeft001` reduces `001` to `C3r001`, the restriction of `C3s001` with that language. The two word restrictions differ: our width-10 strip still admits `1001`, while our list adds upper run bounds and longer exclusions. This does not establish logical independence of the open left statements. A wider certificate could combine both lists; that is not part of this release.
- Its note `reading-constraints.md` computes the words `00`, `010`, `01110` and `011110` beside `011`. The whole list is not forced for every right half. It separately computes that `011110` is unconditionally forbidden at width `7`, and that `00` is forbidden unless the readings are eventually white. Masked Jen removes that exception here. These two exclusions are therefore prior computational results, now proved in Lean; the same note lists the upper run bounds beside `001` as sampled, not forced.
- Its note `constrained-left-halves.md` studies the left statements with its particular word restrictions and explains an obstruction using dense periodic free words. Extending that obstruction to the complete strip languages here would require a separate lifting argument. Periodic paths in a strip relaxation alone do not give compatible full histories.

What is new is:
- the period-three path check;
- the language beside `011`, in Lean;
- the upper run bounds beside `001`;
- the statements `C3r011` and `C3g001`, with their reductions.

As far as we found, `C3r011` is the first Lean reduction of `011` to a left statement over a restricted free word.

Nothing here proves `C3r011`, `C3g001` or the absence of period `3`. Separate finite-word counts through width `22` give growth estimates of about `0.105` bit per tick beside `011` and `0.084` beside `001`, from ratios of counts at lengths `24` and `18`. These are not certified entropy values or evidence of a positive infinite-width limit. The unrestricted free-bit channel has `1/3` bit per tick.

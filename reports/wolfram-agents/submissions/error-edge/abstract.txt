Take two configurations of rule 30 with leftmost black cells, read from the left edge as arrays of left diagonals (wheels), and let the error edge be the lowest wheel on which they differ at a tick. We prove in Lean, for any two arrays satisfying the kit's left-diagonal recurrence, that agreement below the edge is kept for ever, so the edge never retreats (agree_step, edge_le); that the arrays agree at the edge one tick later exactly when the wheel below the edge is black (heal_iff), so a white parent stalls the edge (edge_stall) and a black one moves it up (edge_heal); and six laws for the wheels above a heal: the first wheel above the old edge is white if it agrees one tick later (white_above), the second is black if the first two agree (black_two_above); hence after a jump of exactly 2 the new parent is white (jump_two), after 3 black (jump_three), after 4 black one tick later whatever it was (jump_four), after 5 with a white new parent black one tick later (jump_five), and after 6 or 7 with a white new parent white one tick later (jump_six, jump_seven). The proofs unfold the recurrence on a window of at most nine wheels and decide Boolean identities; the module builds in seconds. These are the one-step laws of the left damage front of rule 30, whose measured speed is about 0.2428 cells per step (Grassberger, cited by Wolfram); nothing here concerns the center column or the Prize problems.

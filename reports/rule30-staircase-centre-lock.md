# Can a natural staircase lock the centre into period two?

GC1937 / MI856, 27 October 2026. Owner's question: remove the forced centre,
let the row evolve naturally, and ask whether a staircase endpoint can install
an eternal period-two clock on the actual centre column.

## Scope and answer

A short natural alternating trace is not a forced-wall artifact. For example,
the actual single seed has centre values1,0,1 at times1,2,3 and then1 at time4.
Its left neighbour is1 at times1,2 and0 at time3. These small rows are obtained
directly by hand (rows1..3:111,11001,1101111). This illustrates the local
endpoint mechanism; it is not a claim that a long multi-step staircase has
been found terminating at the centre.

One permanent supporting1-bar is impossible for a finite seed. If a fixed
column stays1 for all future time, the rule forces its left neighbour0,
then the next neighbour1, and continues alternating indefinitely to the left,
already on the onset row. That contradicts finite support. This is the known
period-one proof, not a new theorem. Thus each instance of the fixed-bar
staircase guarantee has a finite supporting interval. It does NOT follow that
the centre must stop alternating at that interval's end: another local context
can supply the next switch. An infinite succession of successful replacements
has not been excluded by the staircase argument.

The official Problem1 concerns the centre of the SINGLE nonzero-cell orbit.
An arbitrary finite seed with an eternal period-two column would refute the
broader finite-seed conjecture but would not alone decide that specific orbit.
A proven eternal period-two centre in the single-seed orbit would refute its
nonperiodicity. Excluding period2 alone would still leave higher periods.

https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09431 ; internal full proof RULE30-PRIZE.md8.76,

## Exact compatibility, without a forced update

Write l,c,r for sites-1,0,1. The genuine rule is c'=l XOR(c OR r).
For a continuing alternating centre:

- At c=1, obtaining c'=0 requires l=1, whatever r is.
- At c=0, obtaining c'=1 requires l XOR r=1: either(l,r)=(1,0),
  the steady-bar case, or(l,r)=(0,1), a possible replacement context.

These are necessary and sufficient one-update conditions, not a claim the
neighbours can be chosen independently or maintained arbitrarily. L997 already
records this same phase identity. A forever-alternating centre from finite
support would need l=0 infinitely often: finitely many such events would leave
l eventually constant1, which the known proof excludes. So it would require
infinitely many replacement contexts, all generated by the actual dynamics.

## A short hand check of replacement, including neighbouring updates

HAND DERIVATION, single-party, no novelty claim. Suppose c(t..t+3)=0101 and
l(t)=0. Then the five actual cells at time t, from-3 through1, must be11001:

1. c'=1 with l=c=0 forces r=1.
2. r'=0 XOR(1 OR x(2,t))=1, whatever x(2,t) is.
3. c'=1,c''=0 force l'=1. As l=c=0 initially, l'=x(-2,t), so x(-2,t)=1.
4. r''=1 XOR(1 OR x(2,t+1))=0.
5. c''=0,c'''=1 and r''=0 force l''=1. But l''=NOT x(-2,t+1), so
   x(-2,t+1)=0. Since x(-2,t)=1 and l(t)=0, this requires x(-3,t)=1.

Conversely,11001 in those five positions gives (l,c,r)=(0,0,1) at t,
(1,1,1) at t+1, (1,0,0) at t+2, and c=1 at t+3, for every exterior.
Thus it sustains four alternating centre values through a moment when the
steady left support is absent. It is a finite compatibility witness, not a
perpetual clock or a claim of its occurrence at the single-seed centre.
Consequently a hypothetical perpetual centre alternation needs infinitely
many such11001 events after onset. Infinite occurrence of11001 by itself is
not sufficient: every intervening tick must also be compatible.

## GC1952 clarification: replacements have bounded gaps; a short clock need not start in rain

27 October 2026. Hand application of the already-reviewed no11 and five-sample
right-gate constraints (GC1941/GC1943); not a new gate theorem or a new experiment.
Record searched: black bars, 3,5,7,9, 110011, then the actual GC1937 hand lemma
and GC1941/GC1943 carrying statements. Numerical-word hits alone are not evidence
that the interpretation below is new. No novelty claim.

Suppose the centre alternates forever after an onset. At each white-centre time
write q_n for the left neighbour. At black-centre times the left neighbour is1.
The required right sample is1-q_n. Therefore the known right-gate exclusions
11 and00000 become the left-sample exclusions00 and11111. Zeros in q occur
infinitely often; consecutive zeros are separated by2,3,4 or5 white observations.
In ordinary time, consecutive replacement events are4,6,8 or10 ticks apart.
Between two such isolated left zeros, the left neighbour forms a black bar of
length3,5,7 or9. Initial/final truncated bars of a finite observation require
the usual endpoint qualification; this statement concerns complete intervals
between successive zeros inside the alternating regime.

This strengthens the earlier explanation that invoked finite support to force
infinitely many replacements. The bounded-gap conclusion needs no finite-seed
hypothesis at all: it follows from actual right-side realizability of the clock.
It does not say every choice of these four gaps is allowed. Longer forbidden
words, including101001, still constrain their combinations. Nor is a switch
between immediate support pairs automatically a switch between whole rain patches.

**Cheap objection to the staircase picture.** At time0 put the finite word
110011 on sites-3,-2,-1,0,1,2, with zeros elsewhere. Every maximal black or
finite white run has length2; there is no alternating length-three stretch
anywhere in this row. Nevertheless its centre has0101 at times0,1,2,3.
This follows directly from the earlier11001 lemma, since the extra cell at2
is arbitrary. The successive centre-neighbour triples are001,111,100, and
the next centre is1. Thus a short natural clock need not begin at the endpoint
of any already-present staircase. This is only a four-value witness, not an
eternal clock or a counterexample to an eventual staircase representation.

**Question → response → challenge → better question.** Does proving every
single-seed staircase ends settle the clock? No: even the initial presence
of such a staircase is not necessary for a short clock. Moreover any eternal
clock would recycle bounded-duration immediate supports, rather than require
arbitrarily long individual bars. The precise unproved step is whether the
necessary11001 replacement events can recur with compatible intervening
dynamics forever from the relevant finite seed. One needs a relation across
events that proves depletion, or demonstrates regeneration; neither follows
from a patch's finite lifetime or from the four allowed gap sizes. No such
monotone quantity or regenerative construction is claimed here. No larger
simulation is proposed merely to count more occurrences.


## GC1953 loop check: a short list of gaps is not a finite-state mechanism

Hand countercontrol only, not a new Rule30 trajectory. Let a binary right-sample
word have successive1 positions separated by g_n, where g_n=5 when n is a
positive square and g_n=4 otherwise. Every gap is4 or5. Thus the word avoids
11 and00000; it also avoids101001, which would require successive gaps2 and3.
The gap word is not eventually periodic: it has infinitely many5s and unbounded
blocks of4s between them. If the binary word were eventually periodic, its
successive1-gap word would be eventually periodic too, a contradiction.
Its complementary left samples would have support bars of lengths7 or9.

This disproves only the inference that these short screens force eventual
periodicity. It does NOT assert membership in the full right-visible language,
compatibility with every longer forbidden word, or generation by a finite
Rule30 seed. Those qualifications are the point of the countercontrol.

Conversely, an eventually periodic replacement-gap schedule would make the
left white-time samples eventually periodic; inserting the forced1s at black
times makes the whole left neighbour periodic. The actual centre is periodic
too, so Jen's existing adjacent-column theorem excludes this for a nonzero
finite seed. This is already in PERIOD-TWO.md/Theorem A and the periodicity
argument of reports/rule30-tick-tock-hand-analysis.md, not a new theorem.

The two cheap ideas have now been tested: finite-duration bars do not imply
a finite supply of future supports, and finitely many gap sizes do not imply
a deterministic finite-memory update. A recurrence needs a *sufficient state*
whose next state is determined without reading unrecorded surrounding cells.
No such state or decreasing resource has been established. DQ-011's earlier
long-memory correction already names this same obstruction.

## 3. Batch 1: twelve doors carried

Each entry gives the statement carried, the argument as read, and the verdict. Status of each carried statement: **PROVED, hand, two-party** (Local's swarm, Cloud's read) unless marked COMPUTED.

**3.1 Door 3, *Entropy squeeze as a reduction*.** Route: prove that some left column of every finite configuration has entropy above the channel bound, and conclude none is clocked. The lemma (clocked ⇒ entropy at most the bound) stands. Read: the route's target implies the goal through the lemma, and is not implied by it, so the target is at least as hard as the goal and uses nothing a direct proof would not. Verdict: carried as a reduction with no leverage; nothing is shut, the lemma is untouched.

**3.2 Door 5, *wall-cannot-reach-low-wheels*.** Statement: in wheel coordinates (wheel j at tick t is the cell at offset j − t), the cell of wheel j at tick t + 1 reads offsets j − t − 2, j − t − 1, j − t, that is wheels j − 2, j − 1, j at tick t. So wheels at most j at every tick are a function of wheels at most j at tick 0, under any boundary condition whose altered cells lie on wheels above j at every tick. Two evolutions (a half-row with a wall, the whole line) that agree on wheels at most j at tick 0 therefore agree on them for ever, provided the wall's wheel index stays above j. That guard is the one-line transfer the card asks for; the kit's `zrow_light_cone` compares two starts of the half-row and does not state it. Verdict: carried with the guard.

**3.3 Door 36, *band-crosses-centre*.** The card records a two-party audit before the swarm and asks only for promotion: band membership compares along a diagonal, not a column, so the band's position contradicts nothing, and the band's return to the centre is unproved. Not re-read here. Verdict: promoted on the card's record; the route is closed as an argument.

**3.4 Door 38, *row-periods-to-centre*.** Control: for any sequence c in {0,1}^ℕ put R_j(t) = c(j) when t ≡ j mod 2^(j+1) and 0 otherwise. Since j < 2^(j+1), row j is periodic with period dividing 2^(j+1) and R_j(j) = c(j). So the diagonal of an array whose row j has period 2^(j+1) is arbitrary: the periods of the right diagonals alone say nothing about the centre column. (For exact least period 2^(j+1) at j ≥ 1, also set R_j(t) = 1 at t ≡ j + 1; two adjacent residues in a cycle of length at least 4 have no rotation symmetry.) A proof must use the recurrence between adjacent diagonals, not their periods. Verdict: carried; SHUT for the class "row periods alone".

**3.5 Door 26, *bowtie* (Mahler).** A past window joined to a future window of a deterministic forward orbit is one longer future window from the earlier state, so it pins nothing new. Verdict: carried as a remark; outside this programme's prizes.

**3.6 Door 41, *randomness of the column*.** The centre column is computable from n, so its first n cells have Kolmogorov complexity at most log₂ n + O(1): it is not random in the algorithmic sense. In the statistical sense, randomness (normality) is stronger than Problem 2, which is stronger than what Problem 1 needs. Verdict: carried; the route is refuted in one sense and circular in the other.

**3.7 Door 43, *time-shortcut*.** A fast algorithm for c(n) decides neither question: a periodic sequence, the Thue–Morse sequence (not eventually periodic, density 1/2) and the indicator of the powers of two (not eventually periodic, density 0) all have algorithms logarithmic in n. The kit's implication runs the other way (Problem 3 asserts no shortcut). Verdict: carried; SHUT for the class "a shortcut alone".

**3.8 Door 61, *sieve axioms from low-order data*.** L2-F1, read: for m checks on n uniform bits, the number of inputs passing all checks is 2^(n−m) Σ_A (−1)^|A| b_A with b_A the bias of the sum of the checks in A (expand each indicator as (1 − (−1)^c)/2). With c_1..c_(m−1) independent fair bits and c_m = e + c_1 + … + c_(m−1), every nonempty proper A has b_A = 0 and b_[m] = (−1)^e, so the count is 2^(n−m+1) when e ≡ m mod 2 and 0 otherwise: two systems with identical biases below order m, one empty and one not. L2-F2, read: a triangular system y_(2s) = 1 + P_(2s)(y_0..y_(2s−1)), y_0 = 1 fixes one even bit per check, so it has the exact range law S_W(t) = 2^(W−⌊t/2⌋−1) for t < W whatever the P; with P = 0 the limit set (all even bits 1) holds no natural, with P = 1 for s ≥ 1 it holds the natural 1. So neither the exact range law nor the biases below order m can decide Q01. Verdict: carried; SHUT for arguments whose only inputs are those two.

**3.9 Door 69, *expsum-magnitude-only*, and door 72, *Exponential sums*.** Translate lemma, read: in a finite abelian group G, the translates B + g have the Fourier magnitudes of B, and Σ_g |A ∩ (B + g)| = |A||B|, so some translate meets A in at least |A||B|/|G| > 0 points. Any upper bound for |A ∩ B| that depends only on the sizes and on the magnitudes |Â|, |B̂| holds for every translate, hence is at least 1 whenever A and B are nonempty. No such bound proves a count is zero. With L2-F1 this states the excluded class for door 72's re-judging: bias-magnitude hypotheses cannot reach N = 0. Verdict: 69 SHUT for its class; 72 narrowed to arguments that use signs or structure.

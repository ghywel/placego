# The root as a junction between past and future

GC2025, 28 October 2026. Owner's pull: a hole in an infinite background looks like a drain, joining an
upward ancestry region to the usual downward pyramid. What about time infinite in both directions but
bounded spatial width? Conceptual/hand synthesis, no experiment or novelty claim.

Record read: GC1981–84 in rule30-missing-parents.md; GC2019–22's background-relative fronts;

The ordinary single-seed problem already has a full infinite spatial row. Its white exterior is known,
not absent. A/B change the background and extend the history before the chosen time origin. The full row
at time zero, rather than its black cell alone, specifies the shared future. A and B are forgotten alternatives:
all black maps to all white, and making B's one white hole leaves precisely one black output. There is no
conserved fluid, black-cell number or reservoir of independent bits passing through that cell. An infinite
constant or periodic tail can have a finite description. "Irregular" is not proved random.

Three readings of bounded sides:

1. **Finite black support in the full white world.** The already second-read endpoint lemma gives
   L_(t+1)=L_t−1 and R_(t+1)=R_t+1 for any nonempty finite black support. Its span grows by 2. Therefore
   no nonzero isolated pattern remains in a bounded-width strip, even one allowed to move. Moreover,
   a nonempty span-w row has at most floor((w−1)/2) backward updates staying finitely supported. A full
   past must leave this class; that is exactly what happens at the root. This is a consequence of the
   existing lemma, not a new computational finding.
2. **Observation strip in the unbounded world.** It can extend through all time, but exterior neighbour
   bits remain inputs at its sides. Its W visible bits are not a closed state. Finite observation width
   alone cannot justify a 2^W-state repetition argument.
3. **Closed width-W ring, or finite deterministic autonomous boundary convention.** The complete state
   has at most 2^W possibilities for a ring. A history defined at every integer time must lie on a cycle,
   hence is periodic from every time, not merely eventually periodic. For any time t, inspect 2^W+1
   preceding states: a repetition traps the later trajectory on that cycle, including t. Every time
   therefore lies on the same cycle. This elementary finite-state argument is a PROOF-SKETCH here,
   not a claim of a new theorem. A ring is also an exactly spatially periodic full-line configuration;
   it retains the local Rule 30 rule but restricts the global worlds. A finite crop with forced edges
   generally changes the boundary updates instead.

This identifies boundary conditions and closure as the useful distinction, rather than an extra dimension
or a larger cardinality of infinity. A finite ring can reproduce a finite central time window before its
boundary enters the backwards causal cone; equality on a radius-T input neighbourhood guarantees equality
at the centre for T updates. Its later cyclicity does not transfer to the singleton centre for all time.
Local's ring-orbit work is consequently relevant as a separate exact class, not automatically a prize proof.

Sharper open question: what boundary histories suffice to embed a specified finite strip in a genuine
full-line ancestor of the root, and which strips close into autonomous cycles? First separate compatibility
from closure. No new test, census or theorem is queued; existing inverse reconstruction and ring work are
the immediate sources for that distinction.

An open problem, handed to everyone

$30,000 or ¥120,000,000 could be yours.

Three questions about one simple rule carry $10,000 each from Stephen Wolfram. The Collatz conjecture carries ¥120,000,000 from Bakuage. Rule 30 is a rule for colouring squares: start with one black square, apply the rule row after row, and the middle column comes out looking like coin flips. Nobody can prove that it never settles into a repeating pattern, and the same missing idea stands behind all four prizes.

Since the fourth of October 2026, one person and four AI collaborators have worked on it in public: every claim, every failure, every piece of data in one repository. We did not solve it. We learned a great deal about what a solution has to look like, and we found the exact sentence that is missing. This site hands all of it to you.

Where we stand, in one breath

There is no proof, and nothing to submit. The constant case was settled before we began (Condrey, 2026). The next case, a centre that alternates black, white, black, white for ever, is where almost all our work went. We proved, with a second reader inside the project, that whole families of would-be counterexamples cannot exist; we computed exactly how far any counterexample must sit from the centre; and we found that every route we tried ends at the same missing statement, one of a kind that no field of mathematics has a method for yet.

The one missing statement, exactly

Let R(d) be the longest white run the forced left side can show at depth d, over all real traces beside the alternating centre. Prove that R(d) is finite at every depth d. Finite, not bounded: the data say it grows. A finite pattern with an alternating centre would be white beyond its support, an infinite run, so this one statement refutes period two for every finite starting pattern. In plain words: keeping the centre alternating for ever must cost the pattern real information, and a finite pattern has only finitely many bits to spend. It is proved when the trace is periodic (Theorem B) and for the pure rings the next column can run in; the general argument is open. What is known about it · The lemma in symbols.

The same problem, three times

Rule 30's alternating-centre question has the same shape as two famous open problems in arithmetic: the Collatz conjecture, which carries a prize of its own, and Mahler's 3/2 problem from 1968. In each, a free, chaotic side and a narrow, constrained side must agree for ever. Where the constrained side has only finitely many behaviours, all three are solved by the same argument. Where it does not, all three are open, and for the same reason. The three side by side. Each has its own page: Rule 30, Collatz and Mahler.

Why we are asking

We are few, and the problem is big. Keeping every failure on record is how we did science; it is not a clean handover. This site is the clean handover. If you can read a proof, check one of ours. If you can write one, the missing statement is stated for you. If you have a machine, the census can go deeper. If you know a paper we missed, tell us. How to join.

Who did this?

This project and everything in it were made by one human, Gareth, working with super intelligent systems built by Anthropic and OpenAI: a GPT, two Claudes on his own machines and one in the cloud, each with its own lane, each reading and checking the others, all of it in the open.

Everything here is offered in good faith, and all of it is rightfully subject to doubt. We ask two things of you. Do not take anything here at face value. And do not throw it out because an AI wrote it. Every claim names its source, every hypothesis is testable, every proof is written down to be checked, and every failure is kept on record so that you do not have to repeat it.

Care has gone into more than the mathematics. The problem has been made visible, in pictures and animations, so that a real person with no background can see what is being asked and why it is hard. The Wolfram Rule 30 page is fully readable by anybody with no knowledge of the problem, and Learn starts from nothing.

For mathematicians

The open statements, exactly

This page states the period-two problem and the route we hold in the words a proof would use, says what is proved and what is only measured, and names the section of the record each claim comes from. Nothing here is new; it is the record, compressed.

Definitions

Rule 30 acts on bi-infinite rows of cells x(i, t) in {0, 1} (0 white, 1 black) by

x(i, t+1) = x(i−1, t) XOR ( x(i, t) OR x(i+1, t) ).

It is left-permutive: x(i−1, t) = x(i, t+1) XOR (x(i, t) OR x(i+1, t)), so columns i and i+1 over time determine every column to their left. A finite configuration has finitely many black cells; the single-seed pattern is one. A column is eventually periodic if it is periodic from some time on. Problem 1 (Wolfram, 2019) asks whether the centre column of the single-seed pattern is eventually periodic; the claim is that it is not.

The wall. Period two means the centre column reads 0101… from some time on. Shift time so that it does so from t = 0 and call column 0 the wall, x(0, t) = t mod 2 (phase 0; phase 1 is (t+1) mod 2). Column 1 is the trace, c(t) = x(1, t). By left permutivity the whole left half-plane is a function of the wall and the trace. Since x(−1, t) = x(0, t+1) XOR (x(0, t) OR c(t)) equals 1 whenever x(0, t) = 1, only the samples at white-wall ticks reach the left: the visible word is c at the white ticks, in time order, and the left half-plane depends on nothing else.

Real traces. A trace is real if some configuration beside the wall produces it. The set of real visible words is a factorial language L (one per phase). Its minimal forbidden words are the cuts.

Sources. RULE30-PRIZE.md section 8.13 and 8.54 (left permutivity and the forced left half); CHAT-LEDGER.md CL356 (the masking identity, checked); L640 and L641 (the language and its cuts).

What is proved beside an alternating wall

  • Jen (1990). In a finite configuration, two adjacent columns are never both eventually periodic. So beside the alternating wall, column 1 of a finite configuration is never eventually periodic.
  • Condrey (2026, arXiv:2609.09431). No column of a nonzero finite configuration is eventually constant. This closes period one.
  • Theorem B. If columns 0 and 1 are both P-periodic, the white runs of every left column are at most 2P − 2. Corollary: if the visible word is periodic with period m over a window, the left cells that window determines have white gaps at most 4m − 2 (checked exhaustively for m ≤ 5, tight at m = 2).
  • Theorem A (Jen with a clock). Columns 0 and 1 of a finite configuration can agree with a periodic pair only over a bounded window, so the kicks that break column 1's periodicity cannot thin out faster than geometrically.
  • Theorem A′ (the window principle). A block of column 1 can recur only if its length is small compared with the time elapsed; its complexity bound is Dubickas (2009) in the Collatz setting.
  • Theorem E. Column 1 cannot be a Sturmian word (a coding of an irrational rotation with a half-circle arc); with Jen's theorem, no rotation coding, rational or irrational, is column 1. Corollary F, Theorems E″ and A⁗ extend this to near-squares at unbounded periods, block codes of Sturmian words, resets that come further apart than geometrically, and Thue–Morse and paperfolding for every left edge up to 15,868 cells.
  • The channel bound. Beside the alternating wall, column 1 carries at most 0.1236 bits per visible bit (log₂ of the growth rate of the visible language's layer count at width 28, certified in exact arithmetic).
  • No finite configuration reaches a solid row. The only preimages of the all-white row are all-white and all-black; the only preimages of all-black are the three phases of (100)∞. Neither is finite.

Sources. RULE30-PRIZE.md sections 8.13, 8.54, 8.57, 8.58, 8.20 and 8.33; PROOFS.md; proofs directory entries 05 to 12; CHAT-LEDGER.md CL356 (the Theorem B corollary) and CL363 (solid rows). Proved means a written proof with a second reader inside the project and no outside review.

What is measured

  • The wheel. Beside the alternating wall, column 1 between kicks is the two-arc coding of the rotation t ↦ 17t mod 56: the 56-step word U = 0001001101 0001001101 0001001101 0001001101 0001001101 001101, five blocks of ten and one of six (17 turns in 56 steps). Kicks occur at four classes with short size ranges (Proposition 13). No other column of the single-seed pattern, −40 to 40, shows any such structure (block complexity of a fair coin, exploratory).
  • The rings. Column 1 can run in two travelling waves: the S ring (84 cells, one step is a shift by 14, column-1 word 110100, largest cyclic white gap 5) and the L ring (155 cells, two steps are a shift by 31, column-1 word 1101000100, gap 6). U is made of their words.
  • Nothing near the centre. No period-two counterexample has its left edge within 248 cells of the centre for any right half; none with a right half of at most 32 cells and a left half of at most 108 (4.3 × 109 cases).
  • The record R(d). Exact at every depth 20..120 and at 132, 139, 142 (17 at each of those three), at least 18 at 125..138 and 141, and by sampling at least 20 at depth 200, 28 at 279, 38 at 1,035. Witness configurations for every value are in the trove.
  • The cuts. All 1,130 (phase 0) and 1,199 (phase 1) minimal forbidden visible words of length ≤ 44, each with a cake_lpr-checked certificate; phase 0 extended to 48 with 482 words (in certification).
  • The counting form. N(w, T), the number of configurations on w cells whose centre reads 0101… for T steps, falls like 2w − αT with α measured near 1 at w = 24. The part paid by the seed's left part is proved exactly.

Sources. RULE30-PRIZE.md sections 8.4, 8.13 (the wheel), 8.56, 8.21 (searches), 8.51 (counting); PROOFS.md entry 26 (Proposition 13); CHAT-LEDGER.md CL351 (rings), L643 and L645 (records and cuts), CL357 (other columns); the trove.

The route we hold: finite support

The hypothesis and the target

Suppose a finite configuration has centre 0101… from time 0. Its left half at time 0 is white beyond its support, so its real trace has a left image white on an infinite interval at some depth. Define

R(d) = sup { L : some real trace beside the wall has x(−d, 0) = … = x(−d−L+1, 0) = 0 }.

Target. R(d) < ∞ for every d. This refutes period two for every finite configuration. It is finiteness at each depth, not a uniform bound: the data grow with d.

What is known on this route

  • Theorem B gives it when the visible word is periodic over the relevant window.
  • For the pure S and L rings: a white interval [d, d+L−1] at a white anchor a with N samples of the ring's trace forces L ≤ 2a + 5 or N ≤ d + a + 5 (S), and the same with 6 (L).
  • A mixed prefix LSLSL forces seven white left cells at depths 30..36 (the converse direction: structure in the trace forces white on the left).
  • At depth 139 the record's endpoint is forced by masking: the cell at depth 156 is black from the 78 visible samples through tick 154, and no change beyond site 155 can move it. At depth 132 it is forced by a cut: extending the record word by one white would put the certified cut 100100001001000010000101 at visible index 51 (tick 102). Both are exact explanations of one data point each.
  • Exact images at fixed depth k are regular; a seeded decoder presents them with at most 2 S 2⌈(k−1)/2⌉ states (S the cut automaton's live states), exact in both directions; the first visible bit can be quotiented away for k ≥ 4 in the white-start phase under the no-11 gate. The state space grows with k, so none of this is a closure across depths.

The missing lemma

A white band of length L at depth d forces a pattern in the visible samples over a window that grows with L, and every such pattern long enough contains a cut. Equivalently: the set of real visible words whose depth-d image is white on [d, d+L) is empty for some L = L(d), at every d. Theorem B is this lemma for periodic samples; the two endpoint explanations are what the pattern looks like in two real cases; the general argument is open.

Sources. PERIOD-TWO.md section 2 and row Q6; CHAT-LEDGER.md CL355 (the route), GC1148, GC1151 (rings), GC1153 (mixed prefix), GC1154 and CL356 (Theorem B's corollary), GC1155 to GC1157 with CL356 and CL359 (endpoints), GC1158 and CL361 (decoder), GC1159 and CL364 (quotient).

The other forms of the same problem

  • Conjecture LR. No column 1 beside the alternating wall makes the left half eventually white. Proved for eventually periodic and for Sturmian column 1; open for the kicked wheel.
  • The counting form. N(w, T) ≤ 2w − αT + c for some α > 0. The left part's cost is proved; the right part's is open. Its Collatz twin, with the free bits in place of the left part, is COLLATZ-PRIZE.md section 1.
  • The one sentence. Keeping the wall's conditions costs real information. The delivery side is the channel bound; the cost side is proved only beside a constant wall (Condrey), and beside 0101… it is a coin model, measured and not proved.

Sources. PERIOD-TWO.md sections 2, 5 and 6 (rows Q1, Q6, Q7); RULE30-PRIZE.md sections 8.45, 8.47, 8.51.

Where the problem sits

It has the structure of Mahler's 3/2 problem and of Collatz for a single orbit: a free side (the left half at the wall, a full shift) and a constrained side (column 1, a language of positive entropy but tiny rate) that must agree for ever. The method that closes such problems, pigeonhole on a finite constrained side and injectivity of the free side, is exactly Jen's and Condrey's argument, and it stops where the constrained side has positive entropy. Any theorem about almost all configurations (entropy, ergodic, Fourier) bounds a set and says nothing about the single configuration the prize is about. A proof must use what is special about the alternating wall; the one such structure found is the wheel. The three problems side by side, technically.

Sources. RULE30-PRIZE.md sections 8.45 and 8.47; PERIOD-TWO.md section 5.
